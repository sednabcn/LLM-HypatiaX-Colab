Finding External Reviewers on LinkedIn
A Complete Guide for Academic Paper Review
Quick Overview
This guide provides a step-by-step approach to identifying, evaluating, and contacting potential external reviewers on LinkedIn for your JMLR paper 'LLMs as Interfaces to Symbolic Discovery.' The process takes approximately 2-3 hours to find 2 qualified reviewers who will provide valuable feedback on your hybrid symbolic-neural architecture.
Step 1: Define Your Ideal Reviewer Profile
For your paper, you need reviewers with expertise in at least 2-3 of these areas:
    • Symbolic regression and genetic programming (PySR, gplearn, evolutionary algorithms)
    • Large Language Models for scientific reasoning (GPT-4, Claude, prompt engineering)
    • Scientific machine learning (physics-informed ML, equation discovery)
    • Neural network extrapolation and generalization theory
    • Statistical validation methodologies (non-parametric tests, effect sizes)
Target Reviewer Characteristics:
    • Postdoctoral researchers or Assistant/Associate Professors (more likely to have time)
    • Recent publications (2022-2025) in symbolic regression, LLMs, or SciML
    • Active on LinkedIn with 500+ connections (indicates engagement)
    • Based at research universities or AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind, Google Research)
    • NOT currently serving on JMLR editorial board (potential conflict of interest)

Step 2: Advanced LinkedIn Search Strategy
Search Query 1: Symbolic Regression Experts
Search box: "symbolic regression" OR "genetic programming" OR PySR
Then apply these filters:
    • People (not Companies or Jobs)
    • Locations: United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Germany, Netherlands (major ML hubs)
    • Industry: Higher Education, Research, Computer Software
    • Current companies: Universities, MIT, Stanford, Berkeley, CMU, ETH Zurich, Max Planck Institute
Search Query 2: LLM + Scientific ML Experts
Search box: "LLM" AND ("scientific discovery" OR "equation discovery" OR "symbolic learning")
Apply same location/industry filters as above.
Search Query 3: Neural Network Generalization Experts
Search box: "extrapolation" OR "out-of-distribution" OR "generalization" AND "neural networks"
Focus on researchers working on ML theory and generalization bounds.
Boolean Search Tips
LinkedIn supports boolean operators:
    • AND - both terms must appear: "symbolic regression" AND "machine learning"
    • OR - either term: "PySR" OR "gplearn" OR "genetic programming"
    • NOT - exclude term: "machine learning" NOT "marketing"
    • Quotes - exact phrase: "scientific machine learning"
    • Parentheses - group terms: ("LLM" OR "GPT") AND "symbolic"

Step 3: Evaluate Candidate Reviewers
For each potential reviewer, check these criteria (aim for 6-8 points out of 10):
Criterion	Points
Recent publication (2023-2025) in symbolic regression or LLMs	2 points
PhD in relevant field (CS, Statistics, Physics, Engineering)	1 point
Active LinkedIn presence (posts, articles, comments in last 3 months)	1 point
Google Scholar profile with h-index > 10	2 points
Experience with statistical analysis (mentions p-values, effect sizes, etc.)	1 point
Previous peer review experience (mentions reviewing, program committees)	2 points
Open to collaboration (LinkedIn setting or mentioned in profile)	1 point
Red Flags (Avoid These Candidates):
    • No publications in the last 3 years (likely not active in research)
    • Currently at JMLR or affiliated journals (conflict of interest)
    • Profile indicates overwhelming workload (Chair of 5 committees, etc.)
    • Industry role with no academic affiliation (may lack peer review culture)
    • PhD student or very recent graduate (insufficient seniority for external review)

Step 6: Best Practices & Tips
Timing Considerations
    • Send requests on Tuesday-Thursday (higher response rates than Monday/Friday)
    • Avoid academic breaks (mid-December to mid-January, July-August)
    • Allow 2-3 weeks for review once they accept
    • Send gentle reminder after 1 week if no response to connection request
Professional Etiquette
    • Always personalize messages (mention specific paper or project)
    • Be transparent about time commitment (3-5 hours is realistic)
    • Offer to provide 2-page summary for initial assessment
    • Respect declines gracefully—ask if they can suggest alternative reviewers
    • Follow up with thank you note after review is completed
    • Acknowledge reviewers in the paper's acknowledgments section
Common Mistakes to Avoid
    • Generic copy-paste messages (personalization matters)
    • Sending paper before they agree to review (overwhelming)
    • Asking for 1-week turnaround (unrealistic and disrespectful)
    • Contacting only senior professors (they're often too busy)
    • Not checking recent activity (inactive accounts won't respond)
    • Forgetting to proofread your message for typos

Step 9: Materials to Provide Reviewers
Once a reviewer agrees, send them a package containing:
    16. Full paper (jmlr_paper_with_fig.tex compiled to PDF)
    17. 2-page executive summary highlighting: (a) Research question, (b) Core methodology, (c) Key result (U=0), (d) Main contributions
    18. Assessment document (the one you just received) for context
    19. Specific questions you want feedback on (3-5 focused questions)
    20. Suggested timeline: 'Please provide feedback by [2-3 weeks from now]'
    21. Your contact information and availability for clarifying questions
Sample Specific Questions to Ask:
    • Is the Mann-Whitney U=0 result sufficiently emphasized, or does it get lost in the details?
    • Does the extrapolation protocol (Definition 2, Section 5.2) seem rigorous and generalizable?
    • Are the four contributions (Section 1.2) clearly distinct, or is there overlap?
    • Does the failure mode analysis (Section 9.1) adequately address limitations?
    • Is the paper length (48 pages) appropriate, or should certain sections move to appendix?

Final Tips for Success
1. Quality over Quantity: Two excellent reviewers who provide thoughtful feedback are better than five mediocre reviewers who skim the paper.
2. Leverage Your Network: Check if you have any second-degree connections to potential reviewers—mutual connections can make warm introductions.
3. Be Prepared for 'No': Many researchers are overcommitted. Don't take rejections personally—it's about their bandwidth, not your paper.
4. Offer Value: If appropriate, mention that you're happy to review their work in return (creates reciprocity).
5. Make it Easy: Provide a well-organized PDF, clear questions, and suggested timeline. The less work they need to do to get started, the more likely they'll agree.
