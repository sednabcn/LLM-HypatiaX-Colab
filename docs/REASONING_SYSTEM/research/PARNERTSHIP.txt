Project Title:
Foundations of Automated Scientific Discovery from Axiomatic and Empirical Knowledge
Project Summary:
Current AI approaches to scientific discovery rely predominantly on data-driven induction, even in domains where scientific laws are derivable from explicit axioms, definitions, and structural constraints. Master Research Statement (≈900 words)
Research Interest
My research interest lies in the foundations of artificial intelligence, specifically in automated scientific discovery and reasoning. I am interested in understanding when scientific laws can be derived from axiomatic knowledge rather than empirical data, and how automated systems should decide between formal derivation and data-driven induction.
Background and Motivation
Many AI systems for scientific discovery rely on data-driven approaches such as symbolic regression or neural prediction. However, numerous canonical scientific laws follow logically from explicit axioms, definitions, and structural constraints. Applying data-driven induction in such settings introduces approximation error and obscures logical structure. This motivates a principled distinction between axiomatic derivation and empirical discovery.
Proposed Research
I propose to formalize scientific discovery as a decision problem between axiomatic reasoning and empirical induction. The research will develop automated reasoning methods capable of deriving relationships from axioms with correctness guarantees, and integrate empirical learning only when axiomatic knowledge is insufficient. The framework will combine formal logic, symbolic computation, and machine learning.
Methodology
The project will involve formalizing axiomatic knowledge using logical inference systems, developing proof-based derivation mechanisms, and constructing validation procedures for empirically induced relationships. Case studies will be drawn from classical mechanics, fluid dynamics, and qualitative reasoning domains.
Fit with the CDT
This research aligns strongly with the CDT’s focus on artificial intelligence foundations, reasoning, and trustworthy AI. The interdisciplinary nature of the work fits well with the CDT training structure and supervisory expertise.
Career Goals
My long-term goal is to pursue research in foundational AI, contributing to the development of reliable and interpretable reasoning systems for science and engineering.
