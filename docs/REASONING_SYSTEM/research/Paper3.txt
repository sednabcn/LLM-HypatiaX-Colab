\section{Introduction}
Automated scientific discovery has traditionally been approached as a data-driven problem, where models infer symbolic expressions or predictive relationships from numerical observations.
Recent advances in symbolic regression and neuro-symbolic learning have significantly improved interpolation performance.
However, many canonical scientific laws do not fundamentally require data for their derivation; instead, they follow logically from explicit axioms, definitions, and structural constraints.
Despite this, existing discovery systems rarely distinguish between relationships that are derivable from axioms and those that genuinely require empirical induction.
This lack of distinction leads to two limitations.
First, data-driven discovery is often applied unnecessarily, introducing approximation error and obscuring logical structure even when formal derivations are available.
Second, systems provide no guarantees of correctness, as high predictive accuracy does not imply logical validity.
As a result, automated discovery pipelines conflate axiomatic reasoning with empirical induction, despite their fundamentally different epistemic roles.
In this work, we study automated scientific discovery as a decision problem: given a target relationship and available knowledge, should the system attempt axiomatic derivation or empirical induction?
We propose a unified framework that integrates formal reasoning, symbolic manipulation, and data-driven discovery, and automatically routes problems to the appropriate mode.
When axioms suffice, the system produces formally valid derivations with correctness guarantees.
When axioms are insufficient, the system falls back to empirical induction with explicit validation.
We demonstrate the framework across multiple domains, including classical mechanics, fluid dynamics, and qualitative biological reasoning.
Our results show that several canonical laws can be recovered without data when axioms are explicit, while others provably require empirical input.
By formalizing the boundary between axiomatic derivation and empirical induction, this work clarifies the role of logic in scientific discovery and provides a principled foundation for hybrid reasoning systems.
