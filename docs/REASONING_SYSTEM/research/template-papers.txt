✅ Drop-in abstract (rewritten)
\begin{abstract}
The discovery of analytical expressions from data is a central goal of scientific
machine learning. While many modern approaches achieve high interpolation accuracy
within observed data regimes, scientific models are often required to extrapolate
beyond available measurements and recover structurally correct functional forms.
In this work, we show that high interpolation accuracy is neither necessary nor
sufficient for reliable analytical expression discovery.
Through empirical studies spanning physics, chemistry, biology, and financial risk
modeling, we demonstrate a systematic decoupling between interpolation performance
and extrapolation reliability. Models trained primarily for predictive fit—including
neural networks and language-model-based formula generators—frequently achieve
near-perfect in-distribution accuracy while producing severe extrapolation errors
and structurally incorrect expressions outside the training domain.
We provide a theoretical analysis explaining this failure mode as a consequence of
optimization objectives and inductive biases that do not enforce functional
structure. Our results suggest that extrapolation reliability requires explicit
recovery of analytical form rather than approximation of observed data. We further
identify architectural and validation principles necessary for extrapolation-safe
analytical discovery, and illustrate these principles using a reference hybrid
symbolic framework.
These findings position extrapolation as a first-class constraint in analytical
expression discovery and highlight the limitations of naïve interpolation-based
approaches for scientific inference.
\end{abstract}

5️⃣ Abstract (drop-in, Paper 2)
\begin{abstract}
Financial risk models are often communicated through descriptive natural-language
statements rather than explicit mathematical formulations. Translating such
descriptions into executable analytical models remains challenging due to implicit
assumptions, domain-specific constraints, and the need for interpretability and
regulatory compliance.
We study the problem of transforming natural-language risk descriptions into
analytically correct, closed-form risk models. We show that language-model-generated
formulas frequently achieve high in-sample accuracy while containing silent semantic
errors, incorrect scaling, or violations of financial constraints. These failures are
particularly severe under extrapolation and stress-testing scenarios.
To address this, we introduce a constrained translation framework that combines
language-model-based hypothesis generation with symbolic validation and analytical
execution. The system enforces domain constraints such as no-arbitrage conditions,
probability validity, and dimensional consistency, and rejects formulas that fail
extrapolation-based validation.
Evaluated on a suite of financial risk tasks spanning Value-at-Risk, Expected
Shortfall, liquidity modeling, and decentralized finance mechanisms, the proposed
approach produces executable analytical models that are structurally correct and
interpretable. Our results demonstrate that reliable translation from text to risk
models requires explicit structural validation rather than predictive accuracy alone.
\end{abstract}
