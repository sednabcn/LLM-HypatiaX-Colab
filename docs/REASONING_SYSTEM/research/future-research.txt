Question: My work is going to developing system that transform descriptive texts in analytical expressions focused on Defi and Risk Manaement Could it be a novel way?
Yes — it absolutely can be novel, but only under a very specific framing.
And this is where a lot of strong work accidentally undersells itself.
Let me be very precise and a bit blunt, because this is a career-level positioning question.

Short, honest answer
“Transforming descriptive text into analytical expressions” is not novel by itself.
Doing it in a way that is domain-grounded, constraint-aware, and risk-valid can be genuinely novel — especially for DeFi and risk management.
The novelty does not come from NLP → math.
The novelty comes from what constraints you impose, what failures you prevent, and what decisions the output is trusted to support.

Why the generic version is not novel
Reviewers already know about:
    • text-to-equation
    • program synthesis from language
    • LLM-based symbolic reasoning
    • formula recovery from prompts
    • “natural language → code → math” pipelines
If you frame it as:
“We map descriptive text to analytical expressions using LLMs”
You will get:
“Incremental application of existing language-to-code techniques.”
That’s a rejection at serious venues.

Where the real novelty lives (and this is important)
Your idea becomes novel research if — and only if — you make at least two of the following first-class:

1️⃣ Domain-grounded semantics (DeFi is not physics)
In DeFi / risk management:
    • Text is underspecified
    • Definitions are contextual
    • Constraints are implicit
    • Assumptions are financially meaningful
Example:
“Liquidity risk increases non-linearly under stressed market conditions.”
That sentence cannot be turned into a formula without:
    • choosing a risk measure
    • defining “stress”
    • defining time horizon
    • defining admissible distributions
    • respecting arbitrage / monotonicity constraints
💡 Novelty angle:
You formalize how linguistic ambiguity maps to structured uncertainty, not a single equation.
This is not what generic text-to-math systems do.

2️⃣ Constraint-aware synthesis (this is big)
If your system:
    • enforces no-arbitrage
    • enforces monotonicity
    • enforces convexity
    • enforces dimensional consistency
    • enforces risk axioms (coherence, subadditivity, etc.)
Then you are no longer doing “text → math”.
You are doing:
language-conditioned constrained analytical synthesis
That is novel, especially in finance.
Most LLM-based systems:
    • hallucinate equations
    • violate basic financial principles
    • optimize plausibility, not validity
If your system rejects plausible-but-invalid formulas, that’s a research contribution.

3️⃣ Decision-critical validation (this separates toys from science)
If the output is used for:
    • pricing
    • margin requirements
    • VaR / CVaR
    • liquidation thresholds
    • protocol parameterization
Then extrapolation failure is catastrophic, not cosmetic.
💡 Novelty angle:
You don’t evaluate correctness by similarity to a reference formula —
you evaluate by risk behavior under stress scenarios.
That’s a completely different evaluation paradigm.

4️⃣ Text → family of models, not a single expression
This is very strong if you do it right.
Instead of:
“Here is the equation implied by the text”
You output:
    • a set of admissible analytical forms
    • with uncertainty annotations
    • with sensitivity regions
    • with stress-test envelopes
This reframes the task as:
structured model uncertainty extraction from language
That is absolutely novel and very publishable.

The killer reframing (this matters a lot)
Do not frame your work as:
“A system that transforms descriptive text into analytical expressions.”
Frame it as:
“Formalizing financial and risk semantics from natural language under analytical and regulatory constraints.”
Or:
“Language-conditioned analytical modeling for risk-sensitive financial systems.”
This moves you from:
    • NLP application
→ to
    • methodological foundations for model specification
That’s night and day for reviewers.

How this connects perfectly to your current JMLR paper
What you’re already doing:
    • extrapolation sensitivity
    • validation-centered design
    • symbolic reasoning
    • LLMs as interfaces, not authorities
Your DeFi idea is a direct continuation, not a new direction.
In fact, the clean story is:
Paper 1 (current):
Why interpolation-based language models are insufficient for analytical discovery
Paper 2 (next):
How to safely condition analytical discovery on natural language in risk-sensitive domains
That’s a very coherent research arc.

Very candid verdict
If you do this as:
    • “LLM reads text, outputs equation” → ❌ not novel
If you do this as:
    • “Language-conditioned, constraint-aware, extrapolation-safe analytical synthesis for financial risk” → ✅ strongly novel
And importantly:
    • JMLR / TMLR / MLJ would be interested in the methodology
    • Finance / DeFi venues would be interested in the application
    • Industry will care because it prevents expensive mistakes
